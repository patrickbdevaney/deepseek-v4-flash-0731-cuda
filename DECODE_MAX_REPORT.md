# DECISION DOCUMENT — DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731-REAP, single Thor, batch 1

**Status of the evidence base: every candidate that reached verification was refuted on magnitude, on transfer, or on overlap. Zero findings survived intact. Three survived as *reduced* levers worth building. This document is therefore mostly a list of things not to do, and it is the more useful for it.**

---

## 1. Bottom line

The realistic lossless maximum for this system on the canonical gate prompt is **25–27 tok/s** (from 22.15 today, i.e. 1.82–1.96× over 13.78 tok/s base AR) [EXTRAPOLATED], reached by exactly three orthogonal moves and nothing else: **(a)** one — *one* — draft-head training run worth **+10 to +22% on τ** (2.89 → 3.2–3.5, +2.3 to +4.9 tok/s), not the +32%, +51%, or summed +80% that the individual papers appear to promise; **(c)** a fusion program against the five named glue kernels that make up 57% of the 22.3 ms non-byte term — mHC pre/post (4.94 ms), MoE router+group (3.99 ms), Sinkhorn (1.42 ms), RMSNorm (1.49 ms) — worth **+3 to +6%** of the step [EXTRAPOLATED, after this repo's own trap-3 bench→in-situ discount]; and **(b)** a frequency-ranked trim of the *draft-side* LM head, the only lossless byte cut left anywhere in the model, worth **+3.0 to +3.5%** [EXTRAPOLATED from a 1059 MB lm_head re-read 5× per block against a measured 10.13 ms `fwd_head`]. Composed multiplicatively: 22.15 × 1.045 × 1.12 × 1.035 ≈ **26.8 tok/s**. The single most important caveat, and it dwarfs all three: **22.15 tok/s and τ=2.89 are the worst-case prompt.** The same engine, same head, measures **τ = 3.57–4.00 on a 1023-token code prompt across four runs** [MEASURED, RESEARCH_LOG.md:300], which at the frame's own 7.664 tok/s per unit τ is **27.4–30.7 tok/s already, today, with no work done**. The published intrinsic domain spread for block/semi-AR drafters is 2.1× (AdaFlash, arXiv 2607.19223) [PUBLISHED] and our own 6.11/2.89 = 2.11× lands on it to two significant figures. Before any training run is scheduled, re-baseline against `scripts/prompt_suite.json`; if realistic-prompt τ is already ~3.6, most of the S5 budget is spent before the first gradient step.

## 3. Is fine-tuning the DSpark head worth it, and how much?

**Yes — once, as a single line item, budgeted at +10 to +22% τ, and gated on a measurement that costs hours, not days.**

### Does Nebius's +51% transfer? No. But the reason usually given is also wrong.

The Nebius numbers are real and I verified them: τ 3.20 → 4.79 (KL-FT) → 4.83 (LK-FT) at T=0, and 3.09 → 4.43 → 4.68 at T=1, K=7 eval, MT-Bench + HumanEval + GSM8K, 1 epoch from pretrained MTP [PUBLISHED, huggingface.co/nebius/MTP-DeepSeek-V3-0324].

Three independent subtractions kill it:

**(i) The SFT-distribution component is genuinely spent.** DSpark trained on Open-PerfectBlend prompts with *target-regenerated* responses for 10 epochs (2607.05147 §4.1) [PUBLISHED]. Nebius's chat-template/stale-target failure mode is structurally absent here.

**(ii) But that component was never most of the +51% anyway — show the arithmetic.** The strongest quantitative support for the SFT story is the vLLM/ShareGPT datum: ~83% k=1 acceptance vs ~90% on pretraining-like data. Propagate it. τ = 1 + a₁·S = 3.20 with a₁ = 0.83 ⟹ S = 2.65 for the tail. Repair *only* k=1 to 0.90: τ = 1 + 0.90 × 2.65 = **3.39**, i.e. **+0.19 of the +1.63 gain = 11.7%**. So ~88% of Nebius's delta is later-position repair — which is exactly what the model card itself says ("degraded acceptance rates at LATER DRAFT POSITIONS"), and it is *not* what the SFT argument addresses. [EXTRAPOLATED from PUBLISHED inputs.]

**(iii) And the later-position deficit is demonstrably LIVE in our head.** Finding F45, measured on this engine: feeding real draft proposals back drops acceptance **3.00 → 2.08**, because the shipped MTP heads are trained with a noise-token placeholder, so real proposals are off-distribution [MEASURED, LEVERS.md:187]. That is precisely the free-running mismatch HASS repairs (+8–20% over EAGLE-2) [PUBLISHED, 2408.15766]. DSpark's instruction data fixed the *prompt* distribution; it did not fix the *draft-input* distribution at k≥2 on this checkpoint.

**(iv) The LK loss itself is worth ~nothing to us.** KL-FT 4.79 → LK-FT 4.83 = **+0.83%** [PUBLISHED]. And that +0.83% is a delta over a *plain KL* baseline. Ours is TV-dominant: α_tv = 0.9 of a 2.0 total. By the source's own identity, acceptance α = 1 − TV(p,q) (Leviathan 2211.17192 Thm 3.5), so we already run the acceptance objective. Instantiating Nebius's own hybrid at our operating point: τ = 2.89 at block 5 ⟹ per-position α = 0.692 ⟹ λ = exp(−3 × 0.692) = **0.125**, i.e. 12.5% KL / 87.5% TV — versus DSpark's shipped 10% CE / 90% TV. **The two objectives differ by 2.5 percentage points of mixing weight.** The "+5.6% from LK" recorded in S5_RECIPE.md §2.1 is the T=1 number and is a **net loss** at our operating point: re-baselined with Nebius's own T=0→T=1 degradation (−7.5% for a KL head), our τ at T=1 is 2.67, LK lifts it to 2.82, × 7.664 = **21.6 tok/s < 22.15**. Correct that line. [PUBLISHED inputs, EXTRAPOLATED conversion.]

### What the S5 run is actually worth

The best-evidenced single anchor is the focal + chain ablation (arXiv 2606.11552), and it must be re-derived at *our* block size. The paper is fixed at K=16. Inverting its own equation τ(a,K) = (1−a^(K+1))/(1−a):

| arm | τ @ K=16 | implied a | τ @ K=5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| position-uniform | 2.376 | 0.5792 | 2.287 |
| + decay only (what we already ship as w_k) | 2.583 | 0.6129 | **2.447** |
| + focal | 2.892 | 0.6545 | 2.667 |
| + chain (full) | 3.420 | 0.7084 | **2.996** |

Residual over decay: **+32.4% at K=16, but +22.5% at K=5** [EXTRAPOLATED from PUBLISHED table]. The paper's own K-sweep (Table 6: +8.8% at K=2, +31.5% at K=4, +62.4% at K=8, +72.0% at K=16) routed through the six-benchmark/AIME ratio gives ~**+10%** as the floor. Haircut further for: (1) the paper explicitly scopes its mechanism to *bidirectional* diffusion drafters, where the correct-but-discarded waste pool is 46.9% — a semi-AR chain's is structurally smaller; (2) DSpark's α_conf = 1.0 is already a partial first-error surrogate, so only the focal and chain terms are genuinely new signal.

**Honest band: +10% to +22% τ, central +15%.** τ 2.89 → 3.32; speedup 1.61× → 1.84×; **22.15 → 25.4 tok/s** (band 24.3–27.0).

### What NOT to do

**Do not sum the bucket-(a) findings.** Nebius LK, HASS later-step training, AUF's first-failure CE truncation, D-PACE's dynamic position weights, VSD's acceptance-marginal objective, DBLast's `log r_ℓ` importance term, and 2606.11552's chain loss are **the same lever with seven names**: down-weight supervision at draft positions in proportion to the probability inference actually reaches them. AUF's λ is the hard indicator; Nebius's is exp(−3α); D-PACE's is the differentiable surrogate. No paper in the set runs a head-to-head against an acceptance-aware baseline under matched compute — 2607.01893 concedes this verbatim. Summing them (+51% + 8.75% + 9.6% + 8.6% + 32.4%) is the single most likely way to overstate this program by 4×.

### Hard prerequisite, and it is cheap

Run `scripts/prompt_suite.json` and measure τ across it. Zero training, hours of wall time. **If realistic-prompt τ is already ≈3.5 (and our own four-run 1023-token code measurement says 3.57–4.00), the entire +10–22% band is already banked and S5's marginal value collapses toward zero.** Decide against that set. Never against 2.89.

### Cost gate

Generation-based self-distillation on-device is dead: 800K samples × ~500 tokens = 400M tokens at 13.83 tok/s AR ≈ **330 days** [EXTRAPOLATED]. Draft-OPD's on-policy rollout schedule (16K prompts × 4096 tok × 8 epochs = 524M target tokens) is **274 days at 22.15 tok/s** against a stated "few days" budget. **The only feasible capture is teacher-forced prefill over existing text** (measured 43.8–52.6 tok/s at PS=255–2047), which is why task B9 (prefill optimisation) is the true critical path for S5 — and why nobody has ever run `DSV4_DPROF` on a PS=1023 prefill is the most conspicuous hole in the project.

### Disqualified by arithmetic (no constraint violated — they simply lose)

**Layer-skip / self-speculative drafting, as a class.** The generic form runs a fraction (1−f) of 43 backbone layers per draft token, costing ~(1−f) base steps. Our DSpark head costs **0.104 base-steps/token** [MEASURED], of which ~0.062 is the shared lm_head read any drafter must also pay. The layer-skip drafter's transformer budget is therefore **0.042 base-steps = 1.8 of 43 layers**. Published methods execute 20–30. At f=0.5 the spec step goes 1.804 → 4.02 base steps: **22.15 → 9.9 tok/s.** Break-even needs f ≥ 0.96. This kills CLaSp (1.3–1.7×), SWIFT (1.3–1.6×), KnapSpec (1.47×), ConfLayers (1.05–1.38×), KNN-SSD, CAS-Spec, Kangaroo, HiSpec — *all of whose published speedups are already below the 1.61× we have.* Two aggravations: MoE degrades **fastest** under early exit (Looped-LM 2605.09165: at 10% FLOPs saved, MoE perplexity 75.7 vs dense 55.4) [PUBLISHED]; and no layer-skip result anywhere has been validated on MLA, on DSA, or on hyper-connections, let alone all three — skipping a layer here removes a *Sinkhorn mixing step*, not just a residual block.

**PEFT/LoRA block-diffusion drafting on the shared backbone.** arXiv 2607.12422 is the cleanest negative in the corpus: τ **2.881 vs FastMTP's 1.511** (a +91% acceptance win) yielding **34.05 vs 188.01 tok/s (−82%)**, because draft 50.285 ms ≈ verify 50.621 ms. Even a free-drafting oracle reaches only 67.9. This rules out any S5 variant that LoRAs the 43-layer backbone rather than the separate 476M `mtp.*` path.

**Tree drafting (PCTree, DDTree) on this device.** Re-scored through our verify-cost curve `c_v(k) = 0.692 + 0.308·|U|(k)/6` (the 69.2% k-invariant share is audited: MLA 41.1% + shared expert + compressor + indexer + lm_head): PCTree buys +18.6% τ (9.41 → 11.16) and pays **+42% verify bytes** — net **−17%**. DDTree at N=256 drives the union to saturation, c_v → 8.91, so τ = 10.73 yields 10.73/8.91 = **1.20×, worse than today's 1.61×**; matching today needs τ > 14.2, above any published number at any budget. The theory agrees: the KL acceptance certificate is bounded by log(m+1) while c_v is ~linear in nodes (2606.30265), so **gain is logarithmic in nodes and cost is linear**. Optimal branching at batch 1 on a sparse MoE is m ∈ {1,2}.

**Megakernels (MPK, Hazy, FlashFormer, Ada-MK, AutoMegaKernel).** Measured on this box today: eager 2.185 µs/launch, in-graph **0.501 µs/node**, cooperative `grid.sync()` **0.903 µs/barrier** (flat at 20/40/80 blocks) [MEASURED, sm_110a]. At 2788 nodes the entire remaining dispatch pot is **1.40 ms = 1.96% of the 71.4 ms step**. A megakernel must re-pay ~1075 grid barriers × 0.903 µs = **0.97 ms**. Net **+0.43 ms = 0.60%**, before occupancy loss from compiling every stage at the max register count (`ogroup` 128 regs/33% occupancy vs MoE GEMV 48/75%). *This refutes the project's own F48 arithmetic in both directions* — the barriers are 3–5× cheaper than assumed (0.97 ms, not 2–5 ms) and the prize is 9× smaller than assumed (1.40 ms, not 13 ms). External confirmation on the nearest arch: **AutoMegaKernel on RTX 5090 (sm_120) is 1.23 ms/token vs CUDA-graphed cuBLAS 1.08 ms — 1.13× SLOWER**, because its fused GEMV runs at 63% of peak bandwidth where cuBLAS runs at 90%. On a 12.26 GB/token step a 27-point bandwidth regression is ~20 ms, swamping 1.40 ms of dispatch twenty times over.

**Lossless entropy coding of weights (DFloat11, Huff-LLM, EntroLLM, near-Shannon coders).** This is genuinely a *fifth* mechanism — not speculation, quantisation, batching, or sparsity — and it is the only one I could find. It is dead here by issue rate: 233 GB/s ÷ 126e9 warp-inst/s = **1.85 bytes per warp-instruction**. A 512 B MXFP4 load is 1024 E2M1 symbols; even a LUT-based parallel ANS/Huffman decoder costs ≥4–8 warp-inst/symbol = **4,000–8,000 instructions against a 270-instruction budget — 15–30× over**, before any GEMV FMA. The weaker version was already measured on this box: BN=1 lost 35% (155–160 vs 242–249 GB/s) from ~50 extra instructions per weight load. Compressibility is also small — the checkpoint is already FP8 E4M3 + MXFP4 E2M1, residual entropy ≈8–15% of B_tok. Blackwell's hardware Decompression Engine does not rescue it: DGE lives in the copy engine and **materialises decompressed bytes in DRAM**, turning one 12.26 GB read into read + 12.26 GB write + 12.26 GB read, at 42–462 GB/s with a >4 MB fallback to SM decode.

**Structural sparsity in the shipped MXFP4 experts.** Measured directly on this checkpoint (251.7 MB sampled across layers 0/10/20/30/42 × experts 0/37/99/159): 0.433% zero bytes, **0 all-zero 16-byte blocks out of 15,728,640**, **0 all-zero rows out of 163,840**, 0.000% zero E8M0 scales [MEASURED]. There is no exploitable block structure. Combined with the algebraic argument (y = Σ gᵢEᵢ(x) requires all of w1/w2/w3 for each of the 6) and the fact that union-dedup is already implemented and audited, the answer to *"is there any lossless way to avoid reading all top-6 experts"* is **no**.

**MLA weight absorption.** MLA is 4599 MB = 41.1% of B_tok and is the obvious place to look. Absorption trades KV-cache reads for projection work — but our KV traffic is **~27 MB/token-step at 8K context = under 0.3% of B_tok**, so there is nothing to trade away, and with 64 heads × head_dim 512 and q_lora_rank 1024 the absorbed matrices are *larger* than the factored pair. Absorption **increases** resident projection bytes. MLA's 4599 MB is irreducible without quantisation, as are the shared expert (1082 MB) and the verify-side lm_head (1059 MB): ~9.1 GB/step of hard floor.

**EcoSpec on DeepSeek-lineage routing.** The paper's own DeepSeek-V3.1 row: activated experts **31.4 → 31.2** (0.6%), speedup 1.10× → 1.15×, attributed to balanced routing — versus 1.22 → 1.36× on Qwen3-235B. Fine-grained MoE with 160 experts and small top-k scatters essentially totally. REAP pruning to 160 makes routing *more* balanced. Our own K=5 union is **17.53 of a possible 30** [MEASURED, F64] — the five positions already share 42% of their experts. And EcoSpec needs a candidate set to select over; we draft linearly.

**Domain-specific drafter fine-tuning is NOT net-negative** (this refutes one of our own submitted findings). The "Nepali 15% → 45%" figure does not exist in arXiv 2605.30580 — the paper prints no per-language acceptance table at all, and its Fig. 7 axes start at 0.40. Its corroborating citation contradicts it: 2503.07807 Table 3 shows SFT on reasoning data lifted the out-of-domain "General" bucket **+6.0 pp**. The damaging case in 2605.30580 is a *standalone* drafter distilled on a narrow external corpus; ours is a feature-conditioned embedded head trained on self-distilled target outputs, which is the case 2503.07807 measures improving. The genuine warning that survives: pure narrow-corpus distillation with no replay costs a few pp out-of-domain. That argues for a mixture, not against fine-tuning.

**Measured nulls, recorded so nobody spends a cycle:** Lightning TopK (our `i:topk` is 0.12 ms/step = 5.7 µs/layer, already 2.6× faster than SGLang's optimised 15 µs — reopens only past ~8K context); Flash Compressor (`cattn:compress` 0.05 ms/step = 0.07%); FlashSinkhorn (targets an n×m cost matrix; ours is 4×4); L2 persistence (24 MB persistable against 12–19 GB streamed = a 0.2% cache, dead by ~500×); `CUDA_DEVICE_MAX_CONNECTIONS` (eager 2.203/2.241/2.252 µs at default/32/1 — all within noise; the Jetson Orin Xorg-ioctl pathology does not reproduce on Thor); DVFS (EMC already pinned at 4266 MHz, nvpmodel MAXN); n-gram/suffix drafting (oracle ceiling **+0.0%**, 0 wins in 21 verifies, and the mechanism — trap 27 — is that the draft anchor is *always* the token the MTP just mispredicted, i.e. the least repetitive point in the stream).

## 5. Genuinely unexplored — worth a probe

Ordered by (information gained) / (cost). The first three cost hours and could change the whole plan.

1. **Re-baseline τ on `scripts/prompt_suite.json`.** Zero training, zero code, one checkpoint load. This decides whether S5 is worth 30 h of capture plus a training loop, or whether the +10–22% band is already banked. **Nothing else should be scheduled before this.** [The file already exists.]

2. **Bin measured τ by generated-token index on the 6-token prompt.** Free — the instrumentation exists. DSpark's sliding-window attention is 128 tokens; a 6-token prompt fills **4.7%** of the drafter's receptive field, a 1023-token prompt saturates it. Prediction: τ climbs from ~2 to ~3.5 over the first ~128 generated tokens. If so, 2.89 is a transient-dominated average and steady-state τ is materially higher. **No published measurement of this exists anywhere.** It is a mechanism entirely separate from domain entropy and specific to our 128-token window.

3. **`DSV4_DPROF` on a PS=1023 prefill.** Nobody has ever looked. Prefill measures 52.6/50.3/47.7/43.8 tok/s at PS=255/511/1023/2047 — only **3.4×** the M=1 decode rate, when a forward amortising 12.26 GB over 255 positions should be compute-bound. Every kernel in that path was tuned for M=1: RB fitted to a 1.71-rows-per-expert histogram prefill does not have; the grouped GEMV chosen over the mma GEMM on a decision that inverts at prefill row counts (the GEMM lost 416 vs 783 µs at M=1, but at PS=255 arithmetic intensity is ~150× higher). **This is the gate on S5's feasibility, not a decode lever.**

4. **Audit whether τ = 2.89 includes the bonus token.** SPEED-Bench Eq. 1 (2604.09557) and DSpark both include the free verification token [PUBLISHED]. If our counter does not, our comparable number is **3.89**, our ceiling is 6.0 not 5.0, and every cross-paper comparison in the repo shifts by +1.00. One-line check that changes the sign of several conclusions. Also audit the draft sampler: if we argmax the draft head while the target samples at T>0, our τ at temperature is biased and comparable to nothing.

5. **Re-open B0 on the two `<<<1,1>>>` MoE routing kernels.** F73 declared `k_moe_prefix` and `k_build_tiles` "at the launch-latency floor" at 0.24–0.25 ms / 43 layers = **5.6 µs/layer**. Today's measurement puts the in-graph node floor at **0.501 µs**. They are 11× above it. They are not at the launch floor; they are at their own O(160) serial-work floor. That is a factual refutation of a recorded kill, and it is the basis for lever #3 in the table.

6. **Allocator re-probe at the actual ~100 GiB footprint.** `tools/alloc_probe` stops at 16 GiB. Managed+prefetch loses 13.4% on the streaming column from 4 → 16 GiB where cudaMalloc loses 2.5%; TLB-reach literature predicts the divergence continues. The strided column (what the engine does) ties at both sizes, so my prior is near-null — but this is the calibration constant behind every "% of achievable" in the project and it has never been measured at the working-set size that matters. Cost: one command.

7. **Bare `cp.async.bulk.prefetch.L2.global` one operator ahead.** Explicitly *not* the F81 ring: a bare prefetch has no `mbarrier`, no smem, no ring stage, no register array, and therefore does not touch bytes-per-barrier — the exact quantity F81 traded away. It only adds memory-level parallelism, which is what ncu names as the deficit (long_scoreboard 66–84%, memory throughput 14–25%). Falsification is one measurement: prefetch layer L+1's first weight tensor at the top of layer L and re-check `q:wq_a`'s cold 115 GB/s mark. If it does not move, close permanently.

8. **The α_conf gradient-budget reallocation.** α_conf = 1.0 is the largest single weight in the DSpark objective and its only consumer is a confidence scheduler — which our own measurement shows is worth +9–11% *and is already shipped and banked*. Whether that gradient budget is better spent on the focal/chain terms is covered by **no paper anywhere**. Free ablation inside a training run that is happening anyway.

9. **KV-injection retrofit onto the existing 3 DSpark blocks (DFlash mechanism).** The parameter cost is one new tensor W_c ∈ ℝ^{4096×20480} = 83.9M params = 168 MB BF16; the per-layer W^K/W^V are *reused* for target features, so no new per-layer weights. Injected KV cache: 201 MB at 8K, 805 MB at 32K — fits 13.6 GiB. **No published evidence of a retrofit exists**, and AngelSpec documents no DSpark↔DFlash checkpoint conversion despite shipping both. One datapoint runs the *opposite* direction (DFlash → DSpark warm-start is known to work). Speculative, but it is the only bucket-(a) idea whose payoff could exceed the loss-reshaping band, and it is a competing — not additive — claim on the same S5 budget.

10. **Thread-block clusters / DSMEM are available on sm_110a.** Measured today: `__cluster_dims__(2,1,1)` launches clean, `barrier.cluster.arrive/wait.aligned` and `mapa.shared::cluster.u32` assemble and execute at `-arch=sm_110a`, `cudaDevAttrClusterLaunch = 1`. **This is not in the project's hardware notes.** It does not help cut-T_fixed (a 2–8 CTA cluster claims 10–40% of a 20-SM machine), but it is a prerequisite for TMA multicast and should be recorded.

11. **LPDDR5X read/write bus turnaround.** The 273 → 233–240 GB/s gap (85–88%) is refresh + tRC + turnaround. A decode step that interleaves activation *writes* into the weight read stream pays turnaround penalties. Batching activation writebacks to the end of each operator, or steering them to a distinct region, is a 1–3% class idea with **no published measurement on any Blackwell part** that I could find.

## 6. Where findings contradict the measured arithmetic

**(a) The frame's own headline understates the operating point.** τ = 2.89 is a 6-token trivia prompt. The published intrinsic domain spread for block/semi-AR drafters is **2.1×** (AdaFlash 2607.19223: DFlash ranges 3.40 ShareGPT → 7.09 MathQA; EAGLE-3 only 1.2×) [PUBLISHED]. Ours is 6.11/2.89 = **2.11×**. Essentially none of that gap is head quality. Meanwhile production traffic runs *below* benchmarks by ~1.9× (AngelSpec 2607.25852: benchmark 4.79 vs Hunyuan production 2.50) [PUBLISHED]. So 2.89 is plausibly production-representative and 6.11 is not a target. **Set the S5 success criterion against a Spec-Bench-style 6-subtask mean.**

**(b) The "measured null at batch 1" for confidence-scheduled verify width is wrong twice over.** LMSYS's premise — "the target verify does not slow down much with more tokens" — is false on a byte-bound sparse MoE. F64 measured the routed-expert union directly: K=1→6.00, K=2→9.67, K=3→12.58, K=4→15.16, K=5→17.53. At 1.053 weight reads/expert (RB=4, post-F70) × 13.37 MB × 43 layers: K=5 = **10.08 GB**, K=3 = **7.23 GB**. Trimming K 5→3 removes **2.85 GB of a 12.26 GB budget = 12.2 ms at 233 GB/s = 17% of the step.** And F63 measured the A/B on this box: **+9.4/+9.5%, +8.6/+10.8%, +8.9/+10.8%** on three of five prompts, wash on two, byte-identical outputs (F59). *However* — the bottom line "0% available" is accidentally right, for the opposite reason: adaptK=1.50 is the default and is already inside the 22.15 baseline. And its current worth is lower than F63 measured: the F64/F70 union fix cut the byte lever from 6.90 GB to 2.85 GB per K:5→3 trim, so present value is ~+3.7 to +4.5% where it engages (ratio 0.413). **Open action: re-fit the threshold post-F64.** Note also that prompt 0 — the prompt every headline number is quoted from — is precisely where the lever is a wash (−0.7/−0.2%).

**(c) The 19.0 tok/s "floor" is a normalisation constant, not a target, and the 2.2% remaining-headroom claim may be too tight.** 12.26 GB / 233 GB/s = 52.6 ms = 19.0 tok/s assumes every kernel moves bytes at full DRAM rate *and* that the non-byte part is zero. Byte-weighting the K=1 dprof marks gives a byte-mark average of **191 GB/s, not 233**, and 22.3 ms of the 71.4 ms step that is not bytes at all. Realistic floor 69.8 ms = 14.33 tok/s; now 71.4 ms = 14.01. Hence 2.2% realistic / 12.3% optimistic. **The external calibration argues this is too pessimistic**: llama.cpp on GB10/DGX Spark (sm_121, 128 GB LPDDR5X, same 273 GB/s spec) sustains Qwen3-32B at 10.7 tok/s and Qwen3-8B at 43.7 tok/s at batch 1, implying **~199–220 GB/s end-to-end effective = 73–81% of spec** [EXTRAPOLATED from PUBLISHED tok/s + estimated Q4_K_M file sizes]. This engine runs 12.26 GB / 71.4 ms = **171.7 GB/s = 63% of spec**. The honest counter is that llama.cpp's dense models have no MLA, no DSA indexer, no hyper-connections, no 20-iteration Sinkhorn and no 160-expert routing — a real part of our 22.3 ms is genuinely architectural. But **16–28% is the correct gap to argue against, and it is larger than the 2.2%/12.3% the repo currently claims.** GB10's own MoE cell (Qwen3-30B-A3B at 89.3 tok/s) shows MoE decode on this hardware class is nowhere near a wall.

**(d) T_fixed is not launch-bound, and the whole bucket-(c) program has been aimed at the wrong term.** Decomposition of the 22.3 ms from `evidence/dprof6.log` (K=1, scaled ×0.936 to the current step): in-graph dispatch **1.40 ms (6%)**; mHC pre+post **4.94 ms** (of which Sinkhorn 1.42); RMSNorm **1.49 ms**; MoE router+group **3.99 ms**; MoE act+combine 0.82; cattn sparse+compress 0.76; kv xin copy 0.18. Named glue = **12.7 ms = 57% of T_fixed**. The residual ~8.2 ms is intra-kernel latency inside byte-moving GEMVs (ncu: long_scoreboard 66–84%), which is bucket (b)/(a) territory. **Every one of the five glue kernels is a 1–4-SM kernel serialized behind the next on a 20-SM device — a CUDA graph does not remove that, only fusion does.** This is why B4's kill ("fuse the elementwise glue, ≤0.4%") does not cover levers #2, #3 and #5: B4 priced by *bytes moved* and then applied the 1.05× verify-graph cap, but the verify-graph cap measures launch cost only, and these kernels' cost is ramp-up and drain, not dispatch.

**(e) Nebius's headline decomposed against its own supporting datum** — shown in §3(ii): the mechanism the refutation named accounts for **11.7%** of the +51%, not 100%. And the LK loss itself, correctly re-baselined against a TV-dominant objective at λ = exp(−3 × 0.692) = 0.125 vs our 0.10, is worth ~**+0.1%**, with the T=1 arm **negative** (21.6 tok/s vs 22.15).

**(f) Block-size correction on every τ number in the corpus.** The papers measure at K=16 (2606.11552, 2607.01893 B=16, DBLast B=15), K=10 (D-PACE Llama), K=7 (Nebius). We run 5. Re-derived at K=5 via τ(a,K) = (1−a^(K+1))/(1−a): 2606.11552's +32.4% becomes **+22.5%**; 2607.01893's +8.75% becomes **+2.5%** (after also switching from its mask-only DFlash number to its own semi-AR Domino transfer, +4.69%, since DSpark has the intra-block conditioning channel AUF exists to substitute for — and dividing by the 1.86× shrink in misaligned CE mass, 70.0% at B=16/α=0.583 → 37.7% at B=5/α=0.692); DBLast's +5–7% becomes **+2.4 to +3.0%** (transplanting its measured Δα = +0.0146, since dτ/dα ≈ 1/(1−α)² is ~39 at their α=0.84/B=15 and ~6 at ours). **Any τ percentage quoted without its K is meaningless and is systematically inflated for us.**

**(g) Raising the block size is the τ lever nobody costed, and the curve favours it.** Our measured verify-cost increments are +109.7/+29.8/+17.4/+21.1 ms for K=1→2→3→4→5: the K=1→2 cliff is ~1.04 base steps, then ~0.16–0.20 base steps per additional token. **The marginal draft token is ~6× cheaper than the first.** DSpark's own accepted-length advantage over DFlash *widens* with block length (+16/15/18% at γ=7, +30/26/22% at γ=15), and a head trained at block 16 runs at block 8 for only 2.3% of τ while the reverse is impossible (2602.06036 Table 8) [PUBLISHED]. **Free recipe item: whatever S5 trains, train it at block 16 even though the batch-1 optimum will land at k=8–12.** This is the one place where "reduce head depth" and "raise τ" point in opposite directions and the arithmetic clearly favours going long: cutting one of three MTP blocks saves 7.1 ms = 2.0% of the spec step, so break-even needs τ to fall by less than 2.0% (2.89 → 2.83), and DSpark's own depth ablation is monotone. Depth is nearly free here (3 blocks = 6.1% of the step); the head's `fwd_head` phase alone is *larger* than all three transformer blocks combined, which is exactly why lever #4 is the cheap byte win.
