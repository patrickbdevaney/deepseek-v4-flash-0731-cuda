## The decode cost model — is the forward pass flat in context length?

Fitting `ms_per_verify = W + K x kv_mid` over **1470** requests with at least 64 completion
tokens, KV depth spanning **71 - 6592** tokens.

| term | estimate | 95% CI (cluster bootstrap over tasks) | meaning |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `W` fixed cost per forward | **136.7 ms** | [133.3, 140.6] | weight streaming; depth-independent |
| `K` per KV token per forward | **0.03034 ms** | [0.02897, 0.03197] | cache reads + DSA scoring |

**Weight term.** `W` = 136.7 ms to move `B_tok` = 11202.36 MB is an achieved 
**81.9 GB/s**, **34%** of the 240 GB/s this box measures as achievable.

That percentage is a **lower bound on efficiency, not an estimate of it**, because
`B_tok` is defined for an M=1 step and a verify forward is wider than one position.
Dense weights (7753 MB of `B_tok`) are read once per forward whatever the block width,
but the routed experts (3449 MB at top-6) are read for the UNION of experts that the
positions in the block route to, which is strictly larger than 6. The true bytes per
forward therefore exceed `B_tok`, so true efficiency exceeds 34%.

**Cross-check on the intercept.** ROOFLINE.md §3 records an independent measurement
on the byte-identical 180B backbone: **126.7 ms per AR step**. The fit puts `W` at
136.7 ms [133.3, 140.6]. A verify forward should cost slightly MORE than an M=1 step, for exactly
the expert-union reason above — which is where it lands. The intercept is not an
artefact of the regression; it reproduces a number measured another way.

**Depth term — the ROOFLINE.md prediction, tested.** ROOFLINE puts KV traffic at
~27 MB per token-step at 8K, i.e. `K` = **1.37e-05 ms** per KV token at the achievable
bandwidth. The corpus measures `K` = **0.03034 ms**, a factor of **2210x** LARGER.

- at   2048 KV depth: depth term =    62.1 ms, **31.2%** of the forward (forward 199 ms)
- at   8192 KV depth: depth term =   248.6 ms, **64.5%** of the forward (forward 385 ms)
- at  16384 KV depth: depth term =   497.2 ms, **78.4%** of the forward (forward 634 ms)
- at  32768 KV depth: depth term =   994.3 ms, **87.9%** of the forward (forward 1131 ms)

> **The prediction does not hold.** Context depth is not free on this kernel, and
> the flat-cost assumption behind the current priority order is only valid at
> short context. The deeper the agentic session, the more of the budget this takes.

**And it is not a bandwidth term.** At the achievable bandwidth, `K` = 0.03034 ms
would mean moving **7.28 MB per resident KV token per forward**. No per-position
state can weigh that: ROOFLINE's own accounting puts the whole cache at ~3.3 KB
per position, and `index_topk` = 512 bounds how much of it attention may read at
any depth. A cost that scales with depth but cannot be explained by bytes is
compute-, occupancy- or launch-bound, not bandwidth-bound.

That is the good news in this finding. A bandwidth wall is physics and caps what
any kernel can do; this is inefficiency, and inefficiency is recoverable. The
natural suspect is the DSA indexer: selecting the top-512 of D resident positions
requires SCORING all D of them on every layer of every forward, so it is linear in
depth by construction while the attention it feeds is not. **This is a hypothesis
the corpus ranks first, not a diagnosis** — confirming it needs a profile of the
indexer against a long resident context, which is a separate, cheap experiment.

Note what hid this. Every microbenchmark in `evidence/` uses a short prompt, and
at short prompts the term is small. It took a thousand requests of real work at
real depths to make it visible.

## Is the depth finding an artefact? — three ways it could be, tested

**1. Mechanical inflation through `tau`.** `ms_per_verify = ms_per_token x tau`, so if
`tau` rose with depth the slope would appear even on a perfectly flat kernel.

| task | n | corr(kv_mid, tau) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `bfcl` | 189 | -0.231 |
| `bfcl_live` | 399 | -0.064 |
| `gpqa_diamond` | 197 | -0.118 |
| `humaneval` | 164 | -0.422 |
| `math500` | 87 | -0.374 |
| `mmlu_pro` | 143 | -0.295 |
| `scicode` | 291 | -0.438 |
| **all** | 1470 | **-0.355** |

`tau` *falls* with depth. The confound runs the other way, so it cannot have created the
slope — it can only have made the measured slope an underestimate.

**2. Cross-workload confounding.** If the slope came from mixing benchmarks with
different prompt lengths and different content, per-task fits would disagree. On the
`tau`-free quantity `ms_per_token`:

| task | ms/token fit | slope, ms per KV token |
|---|---|---:|
| `bfcl` | 37.2 + 0.01377 x kv | 0.01377 |
| `bfcl_live` | 42.5 + 0.01322 x kv | 0.01322 |
| `gpqa_diamond` | 46.4 + 0.01261 x kv | 0.01261 |
| `humaneval` | 39.0 + 0.01327 x kv | 0.01327 |
| `math500` | 40.6 + 0.01372 x kv | 0.01372 |
| `mmlu_pro` | 46.1 + 0.01363 x kv | 0.01363 |
| `scicode` | 40.9 + 0.01341 x kv | 0.01341 |
| **all** | 41.3 + 0.01365 x kv | **0.01365** |

Four workloads with different prompt distributions, content types and answer lengths
agree on the slope. That is a property of the kernel, not of any benchmark.

**3. Endogeneity of completion length.** `kv_mid` depends on how much the model chose to
generate, and harder items generate more — so the slope could be "hard items are slow"
rather than "depth is slow". PROMPT length is exogenous: it is fixed by the benchmark
item before the model runs. Holding completion length inside a band and regressing on
prompt length alone:

| completion tokens | n | prompt range | ms/token slope per prompt token |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 64 - 200 | 469 | 28 - 3492 | **0.01208** |
| 200 - 500 | 274 | 34 - 3207 | **0.01430** |
| 500 - 1200 | 243 | 37 - 2427 | **0.01448** |
| 1200 - 3000 | 199 | 60 - 2862 | **0.01123** |
| 3000 - inf | 285 | 64 - 2592 | **0.01050** |

The exogenous slope reproduces the pooled one. Depth costs what it costs whether the
tokens came from the prompt or from the model.

## Speculative decode — where acceptance actually pays

`tau` is tokens committed per target forward. It multiplies straight into throughput: the
same kernel at twice the acceptance is twice as fast. Because it is a property of the DRAFT
head against a particular token distribution, it is the one number that varies by content.

| workload | n | mean tau | 95% CI | tok/s | forwards saved vs AR |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| code | 455 | **3.054** | [2.890, 3.340] | 16.82 | 67% |
| math | 87 | **3.367** | n/a | 21.83 | 70% |
| reasoning-prose | 340 | **2.774** | [2.731, 2.823] | 15.91 | 64% |
| tool-call | 588 | **3.243** | [3.029, 3.681] | 19.92 | 69% |

A `tau` of t means t-1 of every t tokens cost no forward pass at all. The spread ACROSS
workloads is the actionable part: it says which token distributions the draft head has
already learned and which it has not, and therefore what the S5 capture corpus is short of.

## Levers, ranked by measured headroom

Each row states what the corpus says, and what would have to be true for the work to pay.
Nothing here is a promise: these are hypotheses the data makes worth testing, in the order
the data ranks them.

| lever | what the corpus shows | headroom if it works |
|---|---|---|
| weight-stream efficiency | forward pays 137 ms for 11202.36 MB, a **floor** of 34% of achievable BW | **little** — measured properly at K=1/prompt 0 the engine is at 77% of roofline (`LEVERS.md` §9). Do not re-chase. |
| depth term (DSA indexer / KV) | `K` = 0.03034 ms/token, **78%** of the forward at 16384 depth | removing it at 16384 depth is **4.64x** |
| draft-head acceptance | corpus mean `tau` = **3.077** | tau 3.08 -> 4.08 is **1.32x** |


See `DECODE_ROOFLINE_PLAN.md` for the investigation that turns the depth row into a change.

The two are not additive and not independent: raising `tau` puts more positions through one
forward, which grows the union of routed experts that forward must read, so part of a
speculation win is given back to the weight term. That interaction is measurable — it is
the `W` of a fit restricted to high-`tau` requests against one restricted to low — and it
is the next thing this corpus should be asked.
